# As Steve on TV — Data Strategy & Implementation Specification
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Target repository:** `stvnprkns/as-steve-on-tv` (`main`)  
**Purpose:** Build and maintain a trustworthy Steve index and a reproducible dataset for *Where Have All the Steves Gone?*

> **Implementation principle:** Extend the current repo. Do not rebuild the site, replace the editorial content model, or convert the archive into an autogenerated entertainment database.

**Preserve** existing slugs, published JSON, editorial copy, related entries, collections, route behavior, public manifest contract, and current `npm run validate:content` / `npm run build` behavior. Treat legacy `index.html`, `app.js`, `steves.json`, and `style.css` as inactive prototypes, consistent with the repo README.

### Known ingestion defects to address first

1. `normalizeMedium()` treats `short` and `video` as `commercial`. **Never infer commercial from IMDb `titleType`.** `short` is a film format, `video` a distribution format; mark ambiguity as unknown/manual.
2. `detectSteveName(characters || job || person.primaryName)` finds Steves who are actors/crew rather than characters. **Character matches must come from parsed character credit text.** Self-performances are distinct and require evidence of on-screen presence.
3. `title.principals.characters` is a JSON-like array; parse its individual values, not an opaque string. Handle `\N`, accents, name boundaries, credit annotations, multiple roles, empty fields.
4. Repeated TV-episode credits can yield several candidates for one Steve. Establish canonical character identity across appearances without collapsing legitimate different characters or legacy published entries.
5. Current sample evidence URLs at `example.com` are placeholders, not proof. Flag those records for verification; **do not silently delete or demote authored entries**.
6. Research has no complete non-Steve denominator. Add an independent study pipeline; do not infer screen popularity from the public index.

## 1. Two products, three data layers

### A. Discovery (private/working data)
Source snapshots, normalized titles and credits, Steve candidate extraction, evidence leads, review queues, reject/merge logs.

**Goal:** Find plausible Steve appearances efficiently, with clear provenance. This layer is NOT public and NOT proof of inclusion.

### B. The Steve Index (public, curated)
The existing `SteveEntry` remains the canonical published unit: one editorially meaningful Steve presence in a work/campaign, with explicit links to character identity and appearances if needed.

**Goal:** An enjoyable, author-written, searchable archive of Steve/Steven/Stephen/Stevie across film, television, and commercials. Public records are reviewed, sourced, and rights-cleared; not a copy of an IMDb database.

### C. Steve Studies (private research + published aggregates)
A reproducible study population of **Steves and non-Steves**, using the same selection and extraction methods, plus U.S. Social Security baby-name frequencies.

**Goal:** Answer whether newly introduced fictional Steves became rarer relative to named characters, while accounting for source coverage, decades, media, repeated episodes, and selection effects.

**Hard boundary:** The public index is a convenience sample and must not enter any population-level denominator or numerator except as a separately labeled editorial example or manually validated candidate matched back to a study record.

## 2. Unit-of-analysis rules

Use these concepts consistently:

| Concept | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `work` | One film, TV series, TV episode, or distinct commercial creative | *Stranger Things*; one 1992 brand ad |
| `character_identity` | A character or named persona across credited appearances | Steve Harrington |
| `appearance` | One character's credited/verified presence in one work | Steve Harrington in S1E1 |
| `steve_entry` | An editorially selected public archive entry | Existing `steve-harrington-stranger-things` |
| `candidate` | Unreviewed discovery item | Credit says `Steven` in film X |
| `study_eligible_credit` | A named character credit in an explicitly selected study population | `John Smith`, character credit in sampled work |
| `study_introduction` | A first documented on-screen introduction of a unique fictional character in the study population, with qualified debut date | Steve Harrington in 2016 |
| `birth_name_count` | One SSA name + sex + birth-year count | Steven, M, 1965 |

**Index identity:** Preserve `SteveEntry.id/slug`, even when multiple works share the same character. Use a separate identity/appearance mapping as needed. Do not auto-merge namesakes. One actor can play several Steves. Several actors can portray the same Steve. A performer named Steve can play someone not named Steve.

**Date distinctions:** `work.releaseYear`, `series.startYear`, `appearance.firstAirDate`, `character.firstConfirmedAppearanceYear`, `entry.yearStart`, and `reviewedAt` are different fields. Never silently substitute one for another. If a character's debut is unknown, use `null` and exclude from the primary **new introductions** metric until verified.

**Ads:** Count one distinct creative as one work, and a recurring ad persona separately as one identity. Do not count re-uploads or alternate video encodings as new creative works. If a first-air date is unverified, store a date range or approximate flag; do not invent an exact year.

## 3. Inclusion policy

The site's existing audience-facing scope remains **Steve, Steven, Stephen, Stevie**, across **TV, film, commercial**.

**For the index, include**:
- fiction and animation where the actual character is named a Steve-family name;
- persons appearing as themselves on-screen with a Steve-family first name, clearly labeled `person` rather than `character`;
- branded/commercial appearances with a distinct Steve named character or an identifiable on-screen Steve-family person;
- recurring advertising personas; cameo, guest, and minor characters where name evidence is credible.

**Exclude / do not automatically publish**:
- an actor/crew member named Steve whose *character* is not named Steve;
- behind-camera names alone;
- a `Steven` substring embedded in `Stevenson` or `Stevens`;
- phonetic guesses, uncertain ASR hits, unverified fan assumptions;
- ordinary online-only videos, podcasts, books, games, and stage productions;
- duplicate uploads of an advertisement.

**For the flagship primary statistic, use only fictional named characters**, not actors playing themselves, celebrity commercials, cameo-as-self, documentary interviewees, or ads. The public archive has a broader definition than the statistical series. Maintain transparent reason codes for exclusions.

**Variant policy:** Four individual spelling variants and a combined group. Do not auto-expand to `Stephanie`, `Stefan`, `Stefanus`, `Esteban`, etc. Handle `Steven` / `Stephen` explicitly, no etymological equivalence. `Steve` can be a character's nickname even when their canonical name is Steven/Stephen; track `credited_name` and `canonical_name` separately and avoid counting the character twice.

**Media taxonomy:** Movie and TV labels derive from verified work kind; *never* map every short, video, or TV movie to an ad. A TV movie can be `film` plus a distribution attribute; record rule in methodology.

## 4. Source and license policy (a blocking gate)

| Source | Function | Implementation & publication rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SSA national `names.zip` | Birth counts 1880–2025 | Official annual files; ingest locally with release date, SHA-256, and `sex`. Names with fewer than 5 births are suppressed; absence does **not** mean zero. |
| IMDb downloadable noncommercial files | Film/TV candidate discovery, working study data | Use documented bulk datasets **only**, not website scraping. Its free license restricts noncommercial usage and republication/repurposing into online movie databases. Keep raw data private and **do not deploy an IMDb-derived public database without a proper rights review/license**. |
| TMDB API | Optional cross-reference, images, credits when allowed | Noncommercial API use requires attribution; commercial use requires an agreement; terms restrict caching and AI use. ## 5. Schema evolution: augment, don't replace

Preserve the existing `SteveEntry`, `CandidateEntry`, `SourceTitleRecord`, `SourcePersonRecord`, `IngestRun`, public manifest, and `0001_imdb_ingest.sql`. Add new **optional, additive** fields to existing Zod objects and SQL migrations. Existing JSON must still validate.

### Minimal additions to `SteveEntry` (optional, backwards-compatible)
- `identityId?: string` — canonical on-screen identity shared by multiple entries/works when verified.
- `sourceIds?: string[]` — references into editorial evidence registry.
- `firstConfirmedAppearance?: { year: number; date?: string; precision: "day"|"year"|"range"; verified: boolean }`.
- `representationType?: "fictional_character"|"self"|"commercial_persona"`.
- `productionIds?: string[]` — source-independent work identifiers (not necessarily IMDb/TMDB).
- `imageRights?: { displayAllowed: boolean; licenseBasis: string; attribution?: string; sourceUrl?: string; reviewedAt?: string }`.
- `researchLinkIds?: string[]` — explicit mapping to study observations; NOT automatically joining to study metrics.

Do not require these on existing entries. Avoid duplicating existing `externalRefs`, `evidenceLinks`, `yearStart`, `entryType`, `verificationStatus`, and `sourceNotes`.

### Add operational and research entities (SQL + Zod)
1. `source_snapshot`: source name, version/retrieved time, SHA, original URL, license classification, imported row counts, ingestion script version.
2. `work_identity`: internal stable ID, type, title, release/first-air dates, region/language **when independently verified**, external reference IDs, source provenance.
3. `character_identity`: stable ID, credited/canonical names, normalized variant, `fictional|self|persona`, merge status; no assumption that same name = same person.
4. `character_appearance`: ID, `work_id`, `character_id`, performer ID/name optional, credited character text, source ID, date, named-credit boolean, evidence strength, reviewed state, dedupe key.
5. `source_evidence`: UUID/ID, target candidate/entry/identity, provider, URL or catalog reference, observed assertion (e.g., name/appearance/debut), locator (episode/timecode), evidence type, review status, captured/reviewed timestamps, permitted display/link status.
6. `review_action`: immutable audit action (`create`, `verify`, `reject`, `merge`, `split`, `correct`, `restore`), moderator, reason, before/after or diff, timestamp.
7. `study_population`: ID, population specification JSON, medium, coverage dates, country/language eligibility policy, ranking/sampling method, source snapshot ID, version, approved status.
8. `study_title_selection`: study ID, work ID, inclusion/exclusion reason, sampling stratum, inclusion probability/weight if applicable, metadata coverage.
9. `study_character_credit`: study ID, work ID, credited name, parsed given-name token, variant/control label, eligibility, actor/person ID, appearance/identity link, exclusion reason, source snapshot; **includes non-Steves**.
10. `study_introduction`: study ID, identity ID, first verified appearance year, medium, eligible boolean, provenance; uniqueness enforced by study + identity (qualified first appearances only).
11. `birth_name_count`: name, sex, birth year, count nullable, observation state `reported|suppressed_or_absent`, source snapshot ID.
12. `study_metric`: study ID, year/decade, medium, name variant/control, numerator, denominator, rate, uncertainty bounds, completeness flags, calculation version; compiled/reproducible output.
13. `study_exclusion_log`: study ID, record/work ID, explicit exclusion reason, reviewer or machine rule, date.

**Constraints:**
- Unique `provider + provider_id` where appropriate; never globally unique on title name alone.
- Foreign keys for appearances, identities, study data.
- `published_entry.id/slug` must remain stable.
- Same source inputs + same version => deterministic IDs, same candidate output, no duplicate insertion.
- Review decisions are not overwritten by rerunning ingest.
- Raw licensed payloads/private data are not exported in public manifests.
- Append `0002_*` migrations; no destructive table rewrites.

## 6. Discovery ingestion (Film + TV)

### Inputs
- `title.basics.tsv.gz`
- `title.principals.tsv.gz`
- `title.episode.tsv.gz`
- optional `name.basics.tsv.gz` for attribution/performer resolution
- optional `title.ratings.tsv.gz` for study strata and diagnostics

**Memory warning:** IMDb is enormous. Do NOT load every title and person into in-memory Node `Map`s as the current script does. Use a disk-backed analytical pass (DuckDB via a small pinned Python CLI, or equivalent streaming/partitioned mechanism). Keep TypeScript CLI orchestration and integration with existing Zod/repository infrastructure. Do not commit downloaded raw IMDb files to git.

### Algorithm
1. Import snapshot metadata; validate file headers, expected fields, `\N`, type codes.
2. Parse IMDb `characters` JSON arrays with null handling. Filter to actual `actor`/`actress`/applicable voice-actor performer roles, not arbitrary `job` descriptions or crew.
3. Extract a plausible character given-name with **anchored token matching** and explicit exclusion patterns. Example match `Steve Rogers`, `Dr. Steven Strange`, `["Stevie", "Other Role"]`. Do not match `Stevenson`, `Steven Spielberg as character Bob`, or cast credits `Self` without separate rules.
4. Attach title type and source; `movie` -> film, `tvSeries`/`tvMiniSeries`/`tvEpisode` -> TV, and `short`/`video` -> manual type resolution (do not classify as commercial).
5. Resolve `tvEpisode` to parent series with `title.episode`. Keep individual appearances but group a recurring character to one candidate/identity only with good evidence.
6. Create stable candidate ID from provider+work/series+performer+parsed character identity (hash). Track collisions/aliases; don't derive identity from slug only.
7. Prefer exact matching to existing published entries by known external IDs and normalized work+character+performer, and flag uncertain cases for review rather than auto-merge.
8. Upsert candidate records into existing repository/DB, respecting current editorial decisions. Write ingest metrics and QA report. Never auto-publish.
9. For non-Steve credits, store only the **licensed/private analytical working dataset** in the study pipeline, when permitted; do not create public candidate pages for them.

### QA
- False positive classification set: cast performer named Steve with role Bob; director Steve in `job`; `Stevenson`; a generic `Self`; a short film falsely labeled ad.
- Positive set: Steve as credited character; Steven/Stephen/Stevie variants; compound credits; animation; TV guest; two actors portraying same character; same performer in separate Steve roles.
- Maintain `tests/fixtures/ingest/` with *synthetic* credits for license-safe automated tests.
- Return explicit `nameMatchReason`, `sourceType`, `identityResolution`, and `uncertaintyReason`.

## 7. Commercials ingestion (separate research lane)

No IMDb `short` shortcut. Commercials need an independent `advertisement` workflow.

Store: brand, product category, campaign, spot title, ad creative ID, estimated first aired year/date precision, country/market if verified, running time, on-screen Steve role (`named_fictional`, `named_self`, `spokesperson`, `voice_only`, `unknown`), source catalog ID/URL, transcript excerpt/timecode if legally allowed, verification status, upload-duplicate relationship, media-rights status.

Discover from Duke AdViews, Internet Archive, official brand/agency uploads, and submissions. Use a **manual small batch first (20–30 distinct spots)** to establish the schema and review experience.

A compliant workflow for audio/video **the team has rights to process** may run ASR to find a spoken "Steve". ASR creates a lead only; reviewer must confirm **the person actually referenced** and that the creative is an advertisement. Do not automatically use LLMs on third-party licensed source materials prohibited for AI processing.

**Statistical rule:** No aggregate trend like "ads produced fewer Steves" unless we also have a well-defined sampled population of *all ads* with comparable decade coverage and naming detection. Otherwise show an **archive sightings timeline**, labeled "documented finds", not prevalence.

## 8. The flagship study — Where Have All the Steves Gone?

### Research questions
- **RQ1:** How has the relative frequency of Steve/Steven/Stephen/Stevie among U.S. births changed by year/decade?
- **RQ2:** How has the rate of newly introduced fictional Steve-family characters changed among eligible named characters in a consistently sampled film/TV corpus?
- **RQ3:** Does birth-name decline precede character-name decline? Is any timing lag robust to control names and coverage rules?
- **RQ4:** Does the pattern differ between film and television, or between spelling variants?

### Primary outcome — defined before seeing the result

`new_steve_introductions_per_1000 = 1000 * eligible_new_steve_character_identities / eligible_new_named_character_identities`

For **TV**, introduction year must be the earliest verified episode or equivalent credible debut record. For **film**, the credited original release year is a first-pass proxy, with franchise/reboots reviewed separately. If date or identity uncertainty prevents a credible debut assignment, exclude from the primary introduction series and report excluded share.

**Secondary outcomes (clearly separate):**
- `steve_named_credit_share = 1000 * eligible_steve_named_credits / eligible_named_credits`, by work/release year;
- `steve_work_share = 100 * eligible_works_with_a_steve / all_eligible_works`;
- `total_curated_archive_entries_by_year` (descriptive archive coverage only);
- `birth_name_share_per_100k = 100000 * SSA_variant_births / SSA_reported_same_sex_births`, with the denominator explicitly identified as **SSA reported/tabulated names**, not all U.S. live births unless a compatible authoritative birth denominator is supplied.

Do **not** present raw births or raw appearances as a direct percentage-to-percentage comparison. Their populations are different; compare trends, standardized indexes (e.g. 1960s=100), lags, and controls.

### Research population
- Initial study window **1960–2025** (freeze 2025 data cutoff; do not extrapolate 2026).
- Film and TV **separate panels**. If you combine, show panel weighting and definitions.
- Start with all eligible, adequately covered works in a fixed snapshot, subject to license. Publish exact eligibility and completeness.
- Region: only call results "American productions" when production-country metadata is available and verified. If that is impossible, label a film/TV corpus by the selection method and available language metadata; don't imply IMDb title language alone proves U.S. production.
- Character universe: fictional, named, valid actor/actress credits. Exclude unnamed/background, self, duplicate episodes, and missing-role rows from **both** numerator and denominator.
- IMDb principal credits are not all speaking roles. Label results **among eligible principal named character credits**, not "all characters on screen".
- Handle series vs episodes separately. Do not compare recurring TV episode credit counts directly to film character debuts.
- Set a coverage threshold (e.g. proportion of sampled works with parsable named credits) **in the protocol before selecting/reporting final years**. Report yearly counts and years excluded. Never convert incomplete late years into decline.

### Sampling plan
1. **Feasibility pilot:** 100 films and 50 TV series *per decade* across eligible decades (~1,050 works); stratify by genre and independent popularity metric where available. Its purpose is to audit character-credit completeness and identity matching, **not** estimate the final Steve rate: rare Steve events may make that too noisy.
2. **Main analysis:** process the maximal consistently available film/TV corpus under a frozen snapshot; optionally build a fixed, reproducible, decade-stratified sensitivity sample. Never select works because they contain Steve.
3. Store deterministic random seed, title-eligibility SQL/filter, included title IDs, sample weights (if design uses them), snapshot hash. The same exact records must be reselectable.
4. Compare results by decade with sample-size denominators and clustered uncertainty intervals (bootstrap at work/series level, not at individual credit level).
5. Perform robustness checks: Steve only vs all variants; lead/principal only; excluding post-release resurfacing; TV series debut vs credited-episode rates; excluding ambiguous identities; and control names.

### Control names (research only)
Use John, David, Michael, Robert as contemporary baseline names; optionally add two historically declining comparators chosen **in advance** by a documented SSA rule. Controls do not become public Steve entries.

### SSA pipeline
Download official `names.zip`, preserve checksum/version, parse annual `yobYYYY.txt`, aggregate sexes and variants without collapsing spellings by default. Respect small-count suppression; if under 5/absent, record `suppressed_or_absent`, not numeric zero. Use sex-specific birth shares and optionally combined, always labeling the base. Latest available full birth year is 2025 as of this spec. Explicitly separate birth years from film-release years (adult characters are typically named years after birth).

### Statistics and interpretation
- Publish descriptive rates + 95% uncertainty intervals for the screen sample.
- Correlation is exploratory, not causal. Lagged birth rates can be examined across plausible predeclared lag windows (e.g. 0–50 years at five-year steps), but avoid selecting the most flattering lag without correction/reporting the whole scan.
- Sensitivity-test whether a generic period/age effect applies to John/David/Michael/Robert as well.
- **Do not claim "Steve overrepresented relative to real life" based on unlike denominators.** This requires a credible age-specific real-world name distribution and well-defined screen-character cohort.
- Any earlier third-party "Steven screen shares" are hypothesis generators only, not part of the new findings until independently reproduced.

### Publication gates
Don't publish the principal conclusion until:
- denominators and sample counts exist for every reported bin;
- title- and credit-coverage audit is reported;
- TV character de-duplication and debut-year QA meet defined thresholds;
- charts distinguish verified data, estimated values, suppressed values, and missing data;
- methodology explains rights, sources, sampling, qualifications, exclusions, and name scope;
- story narrative follows the findings, including if the premise is falsified.

Charts must be generated from exports, not from hand-entered values. No speculative or placeholder stats in production.

### Story beats (conditional; not predetermined findings)
1. Real-life Steve baby-name trajectory (SSA).
2. New film Steve / TV Steve trajectory (normalized).
3. New characters vs recurring/long-lived characters.
4. Comparator names and potential lag.
5. Commercial sightings gallery **separately labeled**, never falsely pooled into representative incidence.

## 11. Implementation sequence and acceptance criteria

### PR 1 — Correct and protect current discovery
- Audit/reconcile current public example links, inaccurate commercial classification, producer-name matches.
- Add tests for character parsing, false positives, commercial typing, and stable dedupe IDs.
- Keep existing public pages/content unchanged; output a human-readable review report.
- Existing validation/build green.

### PR 2 — Evidence and identity model
- Add `0002` additive migrations, Zod schemas, evidence registry and identity/appearance resolution.
- Implement moderation provenance and non-overwriting candidate upserts; retain all legacy IDs.
- Candidate review can distinguish **new identity**, **new appearance of existing identity**, **duplicate**, **out of scope**, **needs evidence**.
- Test intentional same-name/actor collisions and series episodes.

### PR 3 — SSA and the study foundation
- SSA importer + suppressed/missing logic, snapshot metadata and checksums.
- Add `0003` study population/credit/intro/metrics schemas and deterministic selections.
- Research-only bulk extraction of Steves **and non-Steves**, with no publication path for raw credits.
- Feasibility pilot generates actual completeness/QA metrics and non-fabricated CSV fixtures.

### PR 4 — Reproducible analyses
- Implement first-appearance resolution, control names, year/decade segmentation, rates, bootstrap CIs, exclusions and QA reports.
- Two panels for film/TV with clear denominator labels; **no commercial prevalence plot** without representative denominator.
- Metrics recreated exactly from frozen snapshot + seed. Unit tests assert denominator integrity, stable IDs, duplicate prevention, missing years, and suppression.

### PR 5 — Data-linked story shell
- Read validated generated study exports with typed loaders and schema.
- Build story's interaction/visual layers only after final metrics pass checks. Show notes for bins with incomplete data; no placeholder production findings.
- Add on-page methodology and index links to curated Steve records.

### Release tests (must pass)
1. Existing published entry JSON remains valid, existing slugs resolve, published counts don't unexpectedly change.
2. Actor named Steven playing Bob -> **zero fictional Steve candidates**.
3. `short` / `video` -> **not commercial without separate evidence**.
4. Repeated TV credits -> one identity, multiple appearances; introduction counted once.
5. Two characters named Steve in different works -> two distinct identities.
6. Missing SSA name/year -> `suppressed_or_absent`, never zero.
7. Missing debut year -> excluded from introduction measure and included in exclusion report.
8. Data exports never include raw IMDb/TMDB credit dumps or unauthorized stills.
9. Every visible numeric finding can be recomputed from versioned protocol/snapshot and has a numerator+denominator+source.
10. Commercial sightings never appear on the film/TV prevalence line.
11. Ingest rerun does not create duplicates or overwrite reviewed decisions.
12. `npm run validate:content` and `npm run build` pass.

## 12. What to actively avoid

- Don't launch with a fake conclusion ("Steve is disappearing" or "Steve is thriving").
- Don't treat a growing curated archive as a measure of screen representation.
- Don't publish raw API/IMDb credit tables as an online IMDb substitute.
- Don't normalize by "number of TV shows" if the numerator is episode appearances.
- Don't equate actor credits with fictional character names.
- Don't merge Steves because their display name matches.
- Don't infer a debut from a series premiere without proof.
- Don't make a marketing claim about American TV from a dataset with unknown country.
- Don't auto-generate Steve editorial copy, Steveotypes, or collections from bulk ingest.
- Don't automatically download/rehost images or ad footage.
- Don't let stats-engine complexity interfere with the existing site.

## 13. Success criteria

**Archive success:** Reliable candidate discovery; transparent editorial verification; 250+ genuinely researched public entries over time; no fabricated evidence; commercial lane is substantive; existing bespoke product experience preserved.

**Study success:** At least one reproducible, well-defined film and TV naming analysis with measured non-Steve denominators, controls, enough temporal coverage for meaningful trends, and public-safe aggregate exports. If evidence is insufficient, publish an honest methodology or investigation-in-progress, not a definitive extinction story.

**Strategic success:** One stable catalog of Steve identities and evidence, one distinct representative research pipeline, and a compelling editorial layer connecting them—without becoming IMDb-lite.
